Folio stage 1

Brief and criteria

Explain the authorised need, constraints and confirmed success criteria in your own words.

Why it matters: A clear brief gives every later decision something reliable to respond to.

Useful evidence: A concise need statement, the confirmed material/folio constraints, and testable criteria supplied or approved by the teacher.

Useful evidence: A typed production-plan table showing task purpose, dependency, tool function, teacher approval and checkpoint; no universal build recipe.

Folio stage 7

Testing and redesign

Record the teacher-controlled test question, repeated results, pattern and one justified redesign decision.

Why it matters: Repeated evidence makes a redesign decision more defensible than one memorable result.

Useful evidence: A test record with approved question and measure, repeated data, mean/range where suitable, pattern/anomaly note and controlled redesign decision.

Folio stage 8

Evaluation

Judge the product, process and personal learning against the authorised brief and evidence, then propose a realistic improvement.

Why it matters: Evaluation closes the design loop by turning evidence into a judgement and a useful next step.

Useful evidence: Environmental, personal/process and product evaluation using criterion–evidence–judgement–improvement, plus one final evidence selection.

Complete the evidence for this stage

Type your responses. No drawing is required in the website. Each field autosaves in this browser.

Ready to save

Support for this stage
  • Use criterion–evidence–judgement–improvement for each evaluation focus.
  • A balanced sustainability judgement can consider material efficiency, sourcing, durability, repair, process choices, people, cost and end-of-life without claiming the project is automatically ‘green’.
  • Sentence starter: ‘Against the criterion ____, the evidence shows ____. I judge ____. Next time I would ____ because ____.’

Add only your own project evidence. Do not include faces, names or other students. JPG, PNG or WebP; maximum 5 MB.

State what the final evidence shows against the brief or confirmed criterion; avoid ‘it was good’ without evidence.
